V Vault
Privacy Policy

Last updated: September 2026

V Vault does not collect, transmit, or share any of your data. There is no account, no server, and no analytics. Everything below explains exactly what stays on your device, and why.

What's stored, and where

Your photos, videos, folders, captions, and your password are saved only in this browser's local storage (IndexedDB), tied to this app on this device. Nothing is uploaded anywhere automatically, and there is no backend server this app talks to.

Your password

Your password is never stored as plain text. It's hashed using PBKDF2 (SHA-256, 150,000 iterations) with a random per-vault salt — only that hash and salt are saved. A forgotten password can be reset via your security question, entirely offline.

Backups

A backup file bundles your photos, albums, and captions as JSON, then encrypts the whole thing with AES-256-GCM using a key derived from your vault password. Your password itself is never written into the backup file.

You choose where a backup is saved — your device, or a cloud drive you connect yourself. The app never uploads a backup anywhere on its own.

Privacy blur

Vault content blurs instantly when the app loses focus, so nothing sensitive is visible in the app switcher or to someone glancing over your shoulder. This is a browser-level deterrent, not an OS-level screenshot block — a determined user could still capture the unlocked screen.

Limits to be aware of

  • Photos are not encrypted at rest in local storage — only backup files are encrypted.
  • Someone with direct access to your device's browser profile could potentially recover unencrypted content.
  • Clearing your browser's site data for this app permanently deletes your vault, unless you've made a backup first.

Changes to this policy

Since this app has no server and no update-delivery mechanism beyond the app itself, any change to how data is handled would ship as a new version of the app, with this page updated to match.

V Vault is offline-first. This page describes the app's current behavior and is not a substitute for reviewing the source code yourself.
